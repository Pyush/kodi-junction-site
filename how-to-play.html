How to play Kodi, the Gujarati cowrie board game

Kodi is a race-and-capture board game from Gujarat, played with four cowrie shells instead of dice. It belongs to the same family as Chowka Bhara, Ashta Chamma and Chauka Bara. These are the rules used in the Kodi Junction app.

The goal

Each player has four gotis (pieces). Move all four around the board and into the center home square before anyone else. The game takes 2 to 4 players.

The board and pieces

The board is a 5×5 grid of 25 squares, traditionally an embroidered cloth. Five squares are marked with an X: the middle square of each side, where each player starts, and the center square, which is home.

  • Outer track: the 16 squares around the edge. Every goti starts on its player's X square and travels counter-clockwise around them.
  • Inner track: the 8 squares around the center, which lead to home.
  • Center home (Maha-Chowki): the goal, square 25 of each goti's path.

Throwing the cowries

On your turn, throw four cowrie shells and count how many land with the open side up.

ShellsNameMoveExtra throw?
1 open, 3 closedIsto1 stepNo
2 open, 2 closedDugga2 stepsNo
3 open, 1 closedTeega3 stepsNo
4 openChauka4 stepsYes
4 closedChomal8 stepsYes

A 2 is the most common throw (37.5%), then 1 and 3 (25% each); 4 and 8 come up 6.25% of the time each. A Chauka or a Chomal earns another throw straight away. Extra throws stack up, so after a Chomal, a Chauka and a Dugga you have 8, 4 and 2 to spend, one goti move for each.

Moving your gotis

Each throw moves one goti forward by that many steps. You choose which goti to move. If none of your gotis can use a throw, it is lost. Two gotis never move together on one throw.

Tohd: capturing and the inner track

Land exactly on a square holding a single rival goti to capture it. That is a Tohd. The captured goti goes back to its starting square, and you get an extra throw.

Your first Tohd also unlocks the inner track. Until you have captured at least one rival goti, your gotis cannot turn inward: when they reach the inner gate they must go around the outer track again. This rule makes every game a fight, because nobody can win by running away.

Safe squares

The five X squares are safe. Gotis of any colour can share them, and nothing on an X square can be captured. Use them to wait for a good throw while rivals pass by.

Jodko: the pair

When two of your gotis stand on the same square, they form a Jodko. While the pair stays together, it cannot be captured and no rival goti can land on its square, so rivals can't finish a move there.

Only one goti moves per throw, so moving either one breaks the pair, and both can be captured again. If breaking it is your only legal move, you must.

Reaching home and winning

A goti needs an exact throw to step into the center home. Each goti that reaches home earns an extra throw. The first player to bring all four gotis home wins.

Tips to win

  • Get your first Tohd early. Without it, none of your gotis can head home.
  • Count your rivals' throws. A goti 1 to 3 squares ahead of a rival is in the most danger: those throws come up 87.5% of the time. 4 and 8 are rare, at 6.25% each.
  • Park on X squares when a rival is close behind.
  • Build a Jodko in front of a rival to block them, and break it only when you need to.
  • Spread your extra throws. After a Chomal, think about which goti gains most from each throw in the queue.
  • Plan the last steps. Home needs an exact number, so aim to sit 2 squares away (the most common throw, 37.5%), or 1 or 3 (25% each).

Playing online

In Kodi Junction's online matches, each turn is timed: 15 seconds to throw and 30 seconds to move. If time runs out, the game plays for you. A player who leaves or stays disconnected for more than 30 seconds is removed, and the others keep playing. When there aren't enough players online, computer players fill the empty seats so nobody has to wait.

Kodi, Chowka Bhara, Ashta Chamma: one family

Families across India play cowrie race games on a square board, under different names and with small rule changes:

  • Kodi (કોડી) in Gujarat, named after the cowrie shells.
  • Chowka Bhara in Karnataka.
  • Ashta Chamma in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
  • Chauka Bara in parts of North India.

They are cousins of Pachisi and Chaupar, and of Ludo, which grew out of Pachisi. If you know any of them, you will pick up Kodi in one game. The biggest differences are usually the board size, the value of the all-closed throw, and whether a capture is needed to enter the inner track.
